What Is Premature Ejaculation?

A sexual health condition where ejaculation happens sooner than a man or his partner would like during intercourse, often within a minute of penetration. Doctors typically measure this using intravaginal ejaculatory latency time (IELT) — the gap between penetration and ejaculation — to separate a genuine pattern from a one-off. Some men also search early discharge treatment or fast ejaculation problem, which describe the same underlying condition in everyday language.

Clinicians generally split it into two types: lifelong PE, present since a man's first sexual experiences, and acquired PE, which develops later after previously normal control. The distinction matters because it points toward different causes — lifelong cases often trace back to how the nervous system regulates ejaculation, while acquired cases are more commonly tied to stress, new health issues, or relationship changes. Understanding what causes premature ejaculation in your specific case is really the first real step toward fixing it.

It's also far more common than most men assume, affecting a large share of men at some point in their lives. That alone makes it worth treating as a medical issue rather than something to quietly work around. And the reassuring part: premature ejaculation treatment today goes well beyond "just relax" advice — it's a structured, evidence-based process that actually addresses the root cause.
